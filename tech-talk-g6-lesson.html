Lesson Plan

Subject / Skill Focus

EFL, Listening (Day 1), Speaking and Writing (Day 2)

Grade / Class

Grade 6, Sections E3 and E4 (2 classes, approximately 45-50 students total, taught on separate days)

Unit

Full Blast, Module 4a (pp. 50-53)

Group Structure

Cooperative learning groups. Six teams: Red, Blue, Green, Purple, White, Black. Four to five students each.

Content SWBAT

Day 1: 1. Identify a speaker's role from context clues in an audio passage. 2. Match a speaker's opinion to a paraphrased statement.
Day 2: 3. Generate and categorize advantages and disadvantages of classroom technology using target vocabulary. 4. Deconstruct a model opinion paragraph into main idea, support, and conclusion; justify a linking word category under time pressure.

Language SWBAT

Day 1: Use the vocabulary set to describe technology in the classroom.
Day 2: Produce spoken and written sentences using linking words for listing, contrast, examples, and opinion.

Prior Knowledge

B1-B2 (CEFR), high-intermediate. Vocabulary and grammar are mostly review.

Materials

Full Blast Module 4a (Student Book, pp. 50-53), HTML lesson tool (TV display), student notebooks, whiteboards.

Day 1

TimeSegmentWhat Happens
5 minWarm-upStudents discuss how they would spend one million dollars given to their school. Teams vote on four preset options (field trips, VR equipment, a swimming pool, a USA exchange program) and may propose their own ideas.
15 minListeningStudents hear five people give opinions on how a school should spend money, then match each speaker to their role and to their opinion. One student is called at random from each team to answer, so every teammate has to know the material.
15 minVocabularySixteen words related to classroom technology. Korean is shown first; students produce the English word before it is revealed.
5 minClosingTwo students are called at random and must correctly answer two review questions before the class is dismissed. The rest of the class quietly reviews with teammates while packing up.
Formative Assessment (ongoing): Circulate at the start of every activity. Known-struggling students checked first, others spot-checked. Books checked during class and again after class; stamps given at both checks. Substandard work flagged for follow-up.

Day 2

TimeSegmentWhat Happens
3 minInstructionsRules explained for the speaking activity: each team will be randomly assigned one piece of classroom technology and must argue for or against it.
10 minSpeaking ChallengeEach team is randomly assigned one technology (e.g. e-books, virtual reality). Teams have one minute to list as many advantages and disadvantages of it as they can.
8 minVocabulary ReviewTeams are randomly assigned a set of vocabulary words and use them to write sentences supporting their earlier arguments.
12 minWritingStudents read two model paragraphs, one for and one against e-books, broken into main idea, supporting reasons, and conclusion. Teams are then randomly assigned a category of linking words (listing, contrast, examples, or opinion) and must defend their category out loud.
5 minMatching Game (if time allows)Memory-matching game pairing English vocabulary with Korean translations.
2 minClosingSame as Day 1: two randomly called students must answer two review questions before dismissal.
Formative Assessment (ongoing): Same circulation and book-check pattern as Day 1.
Unit-Wide Assessment (not part of this lesson's time block): After all lessons in Module 4a are complete, students may join a review game (Blooket) if their work across the full unit is finished and stamped. Game questions are drawn directly from unit content, so results reflect content application skill. Students who have not finished receive ten additional minutes before joining.

Differentiation

NeedResponse
Shy/anxious studentsMay pass their book to a teammate to read answers aloud; the answer remains their own.
High need for stimulationStructural: fast pace, color-coded teams, visible point progression, multimodal engagement (visual, audio, peer talk, writing, speaking).
Visual impairmentAssigned seating closer to the TV screen.
Lower-proficiency studentsChecked first during circulation, given brief one-on-one reteaching or demonstration.
Stronger studentsMay assist teammates once their own work is complete.
ATL Skills (IB framework): Communication, Collaboration, Self-Management.
Key Concept: Perspective (weighing advantages and disadvantages, opinion versus counter-opinion).
